## Governing disposition
HSP is retained as an **advisory discrimination-frontier mechanism**.

Default role:
`HSP -> ELIGIBILITY / DOMINANCE FILTER / ADVISORY CANDIDATE FRONTIER`.

Not granted:
`HSP -> SELECTION AUTHORITY`.

Automatic next-experiment selection remains unqualified unless a later explicit campaign independently earns that authority.

## Law H1 — frontier construction is model-conditional
An HSP/Pareto frontier is only as good as the rival-prediction model used to construct it.

Preserve:
- `DECLARED_PREDICTION_PARTITION != ACTUAL_OPERATION_PARTITION`
- `FRONTIER_OPTIMAL_UNDER_MODEL != WORLD_OPTIMAL`
- `PARETO_FRONTIER != SELECTION_AUTHORITY`

An HSP frontier may be VERIFIED as the frontier **under the declared model** without being promoted to a statement about the real world.

If model adequacy is UNKNOWN, the frontier remains advisory.

## Law H2 — commit predictions before hidden outcomes
When HSP is used for a prospective or blind discriminator campaign:
1. define rivals explicitly;
2. define the candidate discriminators / operations;
3. record predicted rival outcomes or partitions;
4. seal/hash the public plan/frontier before hidden truth or actual outcomes are revealed;
5. only then execute the holdout / actual-operation engine;
6. compare predicted partitions with actual partitions explicitly.

Post-hoc rival predictions are calibration/history evidence only and SHALL NOT be described as blind causal proof.

## Law H3 — model breach is first-class evidence
A mismatch between declared rival predictions and actual operation behavior is not noise to be smoothed away.

When a model breach occurs:
- record the exact rival/operation rows involved;
- preserve the planned prediction and actual observation separately;
- demote any downstream selection conclusion that relied on the false partition;
- update/revisit the rival-prediction model before reusing the frontier for authority-bearing choice;
- retain candidate failure as evidence.

Do not rewrite a failed prediction into a successful frontier story merely because the true operation happened to remain somewhere on the Pareto set.

## Law H4 — actual selection policy stays external to HSP
HSP may remove dominated or non-discriminating choices and expose an advisory frontier.
It SHALL NOT silently choose the next experiment by itself.

The actual selection rule must be external, explicit, and traceable, for example:
- Attention Reservoir portfolio priority;
- a declared worst-case-survivor / information / cost-risk policy;
- operator choice;
- another separately qualified selector.

Record separately:
- frontier returned by HSP;
- eligible candidates;
- selection policy;
- selected candidate;
- why it was selected;
- whether selection authority was actually qualified.

## Law H5 — singleton collapse is not confirmed rival identity
Preserve:
`SINGLE_DISCRIMINATOR_COLLAPSE != CONFIRMED_RIVAL_IDENTITY`.

If one discriminator collapses the declared rival set to a singleton under an unqualified prediction model, the result is not automatically definitive.

Required default guard:
`INDEPENDENT_OR_MODEL_DIVERSE_CORROBORATION_OR_UNKNOWN`.

Without that corroboration, report UNKNOWN / provisional singleton rather than confirmed rival identity.

## Law H6 — same-model corroboration is not independence
Preserve:
`CORROBORATION_WITH_SHARED_MODEL != INDEPENDENT_VALIDATION`.

A second discriminator generated from the same incorrect prediction model may reduce error while preserving the same blind spot.

Corroboration receives independence credit only when its failure lineage is materially different, such as:
- independently authored hidden/operator holdout;
- independently implemented actual-outcome engine;
- rival-prediction model derived through a materially different model/process;
- external observation not generated by the same declared prediction machinery;
- another explicitly justified model-diverse route.

Two checks are not independent merely because they are different operations or were run twice.

## Law H7 — include a simple baseline when claiming selector value
When evaluating HSP as more than a representation/frontier tool, compare it against at least one simple fixed or cheap baseline appropriate to the domain.

Track separately where meaningful:
- identification / discrimination success;
- truth retention;
- model-breach rate;
- cost;
- number of steps;
- false-definitive rate.

A lower-cost HSP result with worse identification/truth retention is not automatically superior.
A frontier/policy may be conditionally useful in one model regime and inferior in another.

## Law H8 — distinguish exact-model value from model-drift robustness
Positive evidence under a calibrated/exact rival model and positive evidence under hidden model drift answer different questions.

Report them separately:
- `EXACT_MODEL_VALUE`
- `MODEL_DRIFT_ROBUSTNESS`

Do not use exact-model holdout success to imply robustness to misspecified rival partitions.

If hidden drift produces model breaches, downgrade the authority of aggressive single-step selection even when average performance remains favorable.

## Law H9 — HSP cannot bootstrap its own authority
Research survival, campaign completion, or 20-pass closure does not promote HSP into selection authority.

Preserve:
- `RESEARCH_SURVIVAL != CANONICAL_PROMOTION`
- `CAMPAIGN_COMPLETION != SELECTION_AUTHORITY`
- `FRONTIER_QUALITY != AUTHORITY_TO_ACT`

Any future HSP auto-selection authority must be earned by a separate explicit discriminator/campaign and recorded as an attributable project-local amendment or canonical process promotion.

## Law H10 — interaction with OARR / Helix / Attention Reservoir
For this project:
- HSP may construct or narrow an advisory discrimination frontier;
- OARR attacks mechanisms/rivals while marginal information remains Pareto-useful;
- Semantic Helix proposes the local scientific successor;
- Attention Reservoir still performs global breadth / portfolio choice;
- project/local integration authority remains distinct from all of the above.

HSP output does not bypass the Reservoir merely because the frontier is small or singleton.

## Law H11 — hard UNKNOWN is lawful under model uncertainty
If the rival-prediction model is materially breached and no independent/model-diverse corroboration is available, the correct result is UNKNOWN / DEFERRED, not forced selection.

Preferred status vocabulary:
- `ADVISORY_FRONTIER`
- `MODEL_ADEQUACY_VERIFIED_FOR_THIS_HOLDOUT`
- `MODEL_BREACH_OBSERVED`
- `SINGLETON_UNCONFIRMED`
- `CORROBORATED_MODEL_DIVERSE`
- `UNKNOWN_MODEL_ADEQUACY`
- `AUTO_SELECTION_DEFERRED_NOT_QUALIFIED`

## Law H12 — durable execution / evidence preservation
HSP campaigns follow the project’s existing durable execution doctrine:
- prefer bounded one-shot runs;
- preserve stdout/stderr and stable receipts;
- record timeouts as route status, not proposition failure;
- do not mutate sealed prior artifacts;
- seal prospective plans before hidden truth;
- preserve candidate failure as evidence;
- read back receipts before claim promotion.

This law reinforces existing execution doctrine; it does not create a competing execution system.

## Current operational consequence
From this point forward in the Microseed project:
1. HSP frontiers are advisory by default.
2. Any authority-bearing HSP choice must name the external selector.
3. Rival-model adequacy must be tested or explicitly UNKNOWN.
4. Singletons under unqualified models require independent/model-diverse corroboration or remain UNKNOWN.
5. Same-model corroboration receives no independence credit.
6. Blind/prospective plans and rival predictions should be sealed before hidden outcomes where practical.
7. Model breach feeds revisit/model correction, not narrative smoothing.
