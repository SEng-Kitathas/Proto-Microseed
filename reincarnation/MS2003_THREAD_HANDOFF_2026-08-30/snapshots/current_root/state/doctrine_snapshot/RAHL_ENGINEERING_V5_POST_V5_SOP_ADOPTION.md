## A. Statement authority classes are mandatory for doctrine interpretation
When a process/doctrine sentence could affect acceptance, mutation, promotion, selection, or burden, classify its role when ambiguity matters:

1. `ADMISSIBILITY_CONSTRAINT` — required to remain inside the declared objective/contract.
2. `STANDING_OBLIGATION` — inherited from real system/user/safety/security/durability/protocol/compatibility/custody/legal/resource context.
3. `QUALIFICATION_RULE` — evidence needed before a claim, authority transition, promotion, or operation.
4. `DEFAULT` — normal starting choice with lawful evidence-based exceptions.
5. `HEURISTIC` — search/attack pressure; no acceptance authority by itself.
6. `TRIGGER` — raises required rigor/depth; not itself a verdict.
7. `SCAR` — retained non-equivalence/failure boundary; not automatically a permanent ban.
8. `RESEARCH_CANDIDATE` — bounded hypothesis/mechanism with no silent promotion authority.

Preserve:
- `DEFAULT != ADMISSIBILITY_CONSTRAINT`;
- `HEURISTIC != ACCEPTANCE_CRITERION`;
- `TRIGGER != VERDICT`;
- `SCAR != PROHIBITION`;
- `RESEARCH_SURVIVOR != UNIVERSAL_LAW`;
- `OPTIMIZATION_PRESSURE != AUTHORITY_TO_BREAK_A_CONSTRAINT`;
- `DOCTRINE != UNIVERSAL_SCALARIZER`.

## B. Conflict / decision precedence
For consequential engineering choices:
1. establish governing intent and standing obligations;
2. resolve or escalate contradictions among actual obligations/owners;
3. define the admissible set;
4. preserve explicit UNKNOWN/residuals;
5. apply defaults and rigor triggers;
6. optimize inside the admissible set;
7. preserve Pareto-incomparable tradeoffs and decision owner;
8. use scars/heuristics to attack the candidate, not to silently override constraints.

Sufficiency precedes minimization.

## N. UNKNOWN and time
